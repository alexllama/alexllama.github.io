My wife got a Cricut to make various crafts. I discovered that you can buy vinyl sheets that adhere to fabric with heat...basically an iron-on but a bit better quality. Here are the shirts I have made.

I could just buy a shirt with the name of my kids' school sports teams, or I could make my own mimicking the Thrasher magazine logo. You won't be surprised to learn which option I went with.

As you may have been able to tell from this website, I have made a number of cigar box guitars. There used to be a club in New York City called CBGB that was instrumental in the development of Punk and New Wave music in the 70's and 80's. Their logo was an acronym for "Country, Bluegrass, Blues" and was all over the place. The original logo also had these additional letters under the CBGB banner that read OMFUG, which stood for "Other Music For Uplifting Gourmandizers." It just so happens that the first three letters of the logo are CBG, so I had to make a shirt with the same iconic font. But what to replace the OMFUG with? I went with "OIMFJ", which stands for "Other Instruments Made From Junk."
{{< figure src="cbg.jpg" caption="" >}}

This one is for my computer nerd friends. Rochester, NY is known as the Flower City, but it is also known as the Flour City. There's a history there, Google it. I wanted to make a shirt to reflect both names without being repetitive. The clear answer was to use a regular expression! This particular one returns true only for Flour or Flower. Then I added City plus Rochester's logo. In hindsight, the 'o' is redundant since it appears in both conditions. It should be '^Flo(u|we)r$', but I'm not making another shirt.
